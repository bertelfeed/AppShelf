## Description

```text
AppShelf puts your Windows apps into folders that open from the taskbar or the desktop.

Click a folder and a small menu opens with your app icons. Click an app to start it. That is all the menu does: the title, your apps and any folders inside it. Everything else lives in the editor, a clean Windows 11 window where you build your folders.

Folders where you need them
Each folder can sit on the taskbar, on the desktop, or both. On the taskbar every folder gets its own button next to your other apps, with a preview of what is inside. On the desktop one click opens it.

Make every folder yours
Choose how many rows and columns a menu has. When a folder holds more apps than fit, scroll through its pages like in Start. Set the icon size for the desktop and the taskbar separately, from 50 to 200 percent. Pick a light or dark menu. Give the folder icon previews of its apps, a symbol or your own picture, in any color and opacity.

Organize in one place
Drag apps into folders, reorder them, put folders inside folders. Shortcuts are copied into AppShelf, so your folders keep working when you tidy the desktop, and moved apps can be found again after updates.

Good to know
The taskbar buttons are AppShelf's own running buttons, so they appear while AppShelf runs in the notification area. Start with Windows is one switch in Settings. AppShelf does not change the Start menu.

Optional: organize with an AI assistant. A separate tool lets Claude or another MCP client sort your apps into folders, and every change can be undone. AppShelf collects no data, and its updates come through Microsoft Store.

AppShelf is open source under the MIT license. The interface is available in English and Russian.
```
